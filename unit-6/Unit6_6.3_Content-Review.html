The Seven Steps

  1. Identify the problem — gather information, question users, identify symptoms, determine what changed, duplicate the problem, approach multiple problems one at a time.
  2. Establish a theory of probable cause — question the obvious; consider top-down or bottom-up OSI, divide and conquer.
  3. Test the theory to determine the cause — if confirmed, plan the next step; if not, establish a new theory or escalate.
  4. Establish a plan of action to resolve the problem and identify potential effects.
  5. Implement the solution or escalate as necessary.
  6. Verify full system functionality and implement preventive measures if applicable.
  7. Document findings, actions, outcomes, and lessons learned throughout the process.

Book versus Objective Wording

The book’s step 6 says “Preventative”; CompTIA’s objective says preventive. Same step.

Performance (5.4)

Technical Overview

Latency is delay; jitter is variation in delay (it breaks voice and video); packet loss forces retransmission. Congestion and bottlenecks appear where many links feed one. Wireless: interference, channel overlap, low RSSI, insufficient coverage, clients disassociating or failing to roam, and band-steering problems. QoS prioritizes sensitive traffic; baselines from Unit 4 tell you what normal is.

Where Students Trip

The exam asks for the first or best tool, not any tool that would work. “Which cable in the bundle goes to office 214?” is a toner. “Will this run support 10 Gbps?” is a certifier, not a tester.
