Everything about taking the N10-009 exam lives on this page and nowhere else in the course. Read it all once this unit; come back to the checklist the week you test.

The Six-Week Audit

Before you book, answer honestly in your notes: Which domain was your lowest on the practice exams? Which unit test took you the most attempts? Which ticket would you still get wrong? Which video did you skip? The answers are your final-week study plan.

Never Use Exam Dumps

“Brain dumps” are sites, files, or people offering real exam questions. Using them violates the CompTIA Candidate Agreement you accept before the exam. Consequences can include score cancellation, revocation of every CompTIA certification you hold, and a ban from future exams. They also do not work: CompTIA rotates its question pools, dump answers are frequently wrong, and the performance-based questions cannot be memorized. Recognize them by promises of “actual exam questions” or “guaranteed pass.” If someone offers you one, decline and tell your instructor. After the exam, you may not disclose its questions to anyone, in any form — including this class.

Performance-Based Questions

PBQs often appear first and take the most time. Read the whole task before touching anything; if one is consuming your clock, flag it, move on, and return at the end. Partial credit is possible, so complete what you can rather than leaving one blank.

Test-Taking Skills

  1. Read the last sentence of the question first — it tells you what is being asked.
  2. Identify the key words: first, best, most likely, NOT.
  3. Eliminate the clearly wrong answers, then choose between what is left.
  4. Do not change an answer without a specific reason.
  5. Flag, move on, return. Leave nothing blank.

Test-Day Rules

You can test at a Pearson VUE test center or online with OnVUE remote proctoring. Either way you need a valid, unexpired government-issued photo ID whose name matches your registration exactly. Online testing requires a private room, a clear desk, a working webcam and microphone, and a system check in advance. Rules change: read CompTIA’s and Pearson VUE’s current testing policies on the day you book, and again the day before you test.

What Passing Candidates Commonly Report

From community experience, not official guidance: they finished the practice exams comfortably above passing, budgeted time for PBQs, and were glad they knew the port table and subnetting cold. Treat anything you read online that suggests unauthorized materials as a warning sign, not advice.

Booking and the Readiness Determination

Ready means 800, twice

Book N10-009 when you have scored 800 or higher on two consecutive full-length practice exams (5.A or 6.A.1). One high score can be luck; two in a row is evidence. If you are not there yet, use the domain breakdown to plan one more week, then retest.

Book through CompTIA’s website (purchase a voucher) and schedule with Pearson VUE. Exam Readiness Day, Tuesday of the final week, walks through booking together.

Final-Week Checklist

  • Two consecutive practice exams at 800 or above
  • Port table and syslog levels from memory
  • Ten subnetting problems in under ten minutes
  • The seven troubleshooting steps in order
  • ID checked against your registration name
  • Test location or OnVUE system check confirmed
  • A full night’s sleep before test day
