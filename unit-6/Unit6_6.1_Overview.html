The last unit is the largest exam domain — Network Troubleshooting, 24% — and the payoff for everything before it. Four meetings this unit: the fourth, the course’s final Tuesday, is Exam Readiness Day.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  1. Apply CompTIA’s seven-step troubleshooting methodology to a multi-symptom outage, end to end
  2. Troubleshoot cabling and physical-interface issues — attenuation, crosstalk, CRC errors, duplex and speed mismatches, bad transceivers, PoE faults
  3. Troubleshoot network-service issues — switching loops, VLAN and port assignment, routing, DHCP, DNS, and addressing faults
  4. Troubleshoot performance issues — congestion, bottlenecks, latency, jitter, packet loss, and wireless interference and coverage
  5. Select the right software tool, hardware tool, protocol, or device command for a problem, and read its output
  6. Evaluate your own readiness against the course’s 800 practice-exam gate and plan the exam
  7. Create a complete troubleshooting plan and ticket for a scenario you have not seen before
Technical Overview

Unit 6 addresses all of Domain 5.0: objective 5.1 (the troubleshooting methodology), 5.2 (cabling and physical interfaces: incorrect cable, signal degradation, crosstalk, interference, attenuation, improper termination, TX/RX transposed, interface counters — CRC, runts, giants, drops — port status, PoE, transceiver mismatch), 5.3 (network services: STP, incorrect VLAN, ACLs, routing table, default gateway, address pool exhaustion, incorrect IP, mask, or DNS, duplicate addresses), 5.4 (performance: congestion, bottlenecking, bandwidth, latency, packet loss, jitter, wireless interference, channel overlap, signal strength, coverage, client disassociation, roaming), and 5.5 (tools and commands: protocol analyzer, speed tester, port and IP scanners, ping, traceroute, nslookup, dig, tcpdump, netstat, ipconfig/ip, arp, show commands, cable tester and certifier, toner, taps, Wi-Fi analyzer, visual fault locator). The unit closes with exam readiness (6.A.0) and the Final Study Exam (6.A.1).

In Plain Terms

Troubleshooting is a method, not a talent. Identify the problem, guess the likeliest cause, test the guess, plan the fix, fix it, verify it, write it down — in that order, every time. You have been practicing it in every ticket since Unit 1; this unit names each step, adds the tools, and makes it fast.

Why We Start Here

Troubleshooting comes last because it uses everything: the OSI ladder from Unit 1, addressing and services from Unit 2, subnets, routes, and VLANs from Unit 3, wireless and logs from Unit 4, and attacks from Unit 5. It is also the exam’s largest domain, so it gets the final, freshest week before you book the test.

CompTIA Network+ Passes at 720

The exam is scored on a 100–900 scale and passes at 720. Because the scale starts at 100 rather than zero, 720 works out to about 78% of raw points. That is a high bar: a student who treats 70% on a practice set as “good enough” fails the real exam. This course’s mastery gate of 800 exists so that your exam day has a cushion.
