How to Read a Certification Text

Read the Exam Essentials first, then the chapter, then the Exam Essentials again. Do the Written Lab as you go. Answer the Review Questions before you look at the key. The reading is where the exam is won; the videos are where it is confirmed.

Terminology Note
The book’s Objective Map (Introduction) lists objective 1.2 under Chapter 10. The content that objective describes — NICs, hubs, switches, routers, firewalls, IDS/IPS, access points, load balancers, proxies — is Chapter 5, Networking Devices, which is where this course teaches it; treat the map entry as a publisher error. Terminology: the book says “Domain Name System Server” and “Dynamic Host Configuration Protocol Server” in full where CompTIA says DNS server and DHCP server; same thing.
Beyond the Exam
Chapter 5’s analog modem, cable and DSL modem, packet shaper, VoIP PBX, VoIP endpoint, and VoIP gateway sections (pp. 169–173) and Chapter 6’s “A Brief History of TCP/IP” (p. 195) exceed the N10-009 objectives. Read them once for context; do not drill them. The port numbers in Chapter 6 are tested exactly as the objective 1.4 table in Section 2.3 lists them.

Reading Notes

One page per chapter, as in Unit 1: Exam Essentials in your own words, every bolded term, Written Lab answers checked against Appendix A (Chapter 7 has two Written Labs; do both), and your Review Question score with the numbers you missed. Add one thing this unit: copy the port table from Section 2.3 by hand onto an index card. Writing it is the first drill.
