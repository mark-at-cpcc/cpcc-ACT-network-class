Welcome to CompTIA Network+ Training. Over the next six weeks you are going to learn how the networks you use every day are actually built and run — and, just as important, how they break and how a technician fixes them. You do not need to arrive knowing any of it. You need to arrive with the text, a working login, and the willingness to put in the hours outside class, because that is where this certification is earned.

This page covers the practical side: what you need, where the course lives, how grading works here, and how we communicate. Section 0.2, Course Overview and Introduction, goes deeper on all of it and is the first thing to read once you are in.

What You Need

  • Sybex CompTIA Network+ Study Guide: Exam N10-009, 6th ed. (Lammle & Buhagiar), ISBN 978-1-394-23560-5
  • A computer with a current browser and the ability to take screenshots
  • Your Brightspace and NETLAB logins, working before the first class

The Two Platforms

Brightspace

Everything for the course lives here: the unit pages, the Course Schedule, the quizzes, the submission links, and the announcements.

NETLAB

Real equipment in a browser. You reserve time, do the lab, and submit screenshots. The pod resets after you; you cannot break anything permanently.

How Mastery Learning Works Here

Every graded item is worth 100 points and is awarded in full when it meets its completion standard. Work that falls short comes back for revision — it is not scored down. Practice sets are repeated until they are perfect. The goal is not a grade; it is a first-attempt pass on N10-009.

How We Communicate

Email and FERPA

Anything about your grades, your standing in the course, or your personal situation goes through your college email — federal privacy law (FERPA) means I can only discuss those things with you, and a college address is how I know it is you. General questions about the material can come from any address. I reply within one business day; if you have not heard back by then, send it again.

Once you are logged in, open Content → Start Here. Read 0.2 Course Overview and Introduction first, then bookmark the Course Schedule — it is the only place due dates live. See you on the first Tuesday.
