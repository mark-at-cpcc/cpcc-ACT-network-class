Last unit was the wire and the model. This unit is the traffic on the wire: the devices that move it, the protocols and ports that name it, and the addresses — IPv4 and IPv6 — that get it from here to there. By Thursday a line of ipconfig output will read like a sentence.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  1. Compare the functions of hubs, switches, routers, firewalls, IDS/IPS, access points, load balancers, proxies, and the other networking appliances and services in objective 1.2
  2. Explain the TCP/IP model and map its layers to the OSI model
  3. Match the common ports, protocols, and services — and state which use TCP, which use UDP, and which are secure — for objective 1.4
  4. Convert between binary, decimal, and hexadecimal and read an IPv4 address, its class, and its private or public range
  5. Describe IPv4 address types (unicast, broadcast, multicast) and IPv6 addressing, shortening, address types, and SLAAC
  6. Implement and verify DHCP (scopes, leases, reservations, relay), DNS (record types, resolvers, zones), and NTP as network services
  7. Identify a DHCP, DNS, or time-service failure from host symptoms and command output
Technical Overview

Unit 2 addresses CompTIA Network+ (N10-009) objectives 1.2 (networking appliances, applications, and functions), 1.4 (common ports, protocols, services, and traffic types), the IPv4 addressing half of 1.7 (binary conversion, classes, public and private ranges, APIPA, loopback, address types), the IPv6 portion of 1.8 (addressing, shortening, address types, SLAAC, dual stack, tunneling, NAT64), and 3.4 (implementing DHCP, DNS, and time services). Instruction covers the function and OSI layer of each network device and service, the TCP/IP (DoD) model, TCP versus UDP, the objective 1.4 port table, ICMP, GRE and IPsec, unicast, multicast, anycast and broadcast traffic, IPv4 and IPv6 addressing, DHCP scopes, leases, reservations, options and relay, DNS zones, record types and resolution, and NTP, PTP and NTS.

In Plain Terms

Every device on a network has an address, every service listens on a port, and three services — DHCP, DNS, and time — make the whole thing usable without a human typing numbers. When a user calls to say “the internet is down,” the technician asks the machine: what address did you get, from whom, and can you resolve a name? If the answer is 169.254.something, DHCP never answered. If the address is fine but names do not resolve, it is DNS. This unit teaches you to read those answers and to run the services that give them.

Why We Start Here

Unit 1 gave you the layers; this unit fills the ones the exam asks about most — Layer 3 and Layer 4 — and the devices that live there. Objective 1.4 alone is pure recall that the exam tests directly, and it is the vocabulary every later ticket uses. IPv4 addressing comes before subnetting on purpose: Unit 3 will ask you to compute with addresses, and you cannot do that until reading one is automatic.

DHCP, DNS, and NTP (objective 3.4) sit here rather than later because they are the three services a technician checks on nearly every call, and because the NETLAB pod lets you configure them this week on real equipment.

CompTIA Network+ Passes at 720

The exam is scored on a 100–900 scale and passes at 720. Because the scale starts at 100 rather than zero, 720 works out to about 78% of raw points. That is a high bar: a student who treats 70% on a practice set as “good enough” fails the real exam. This course’s mastery gate of 800 exists so that your exam day has a cushion.
