Two class meetings this unit — Thursday is Thanksgiving, and that day’s ticket and lab are yours to do on your own. The material is the part of networking you cannot see in a cable: radio, remote access, monitoring, and the paperwork that keeps a network running when the person who built it is on vacation.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  1. Compare the 802.11 standards, bands, channels, and encryption methods, and select antennas, AP placement, and controller options for a site
  2. Read a site survey and explain signal degradation, channel overlap, captive portals, and guest and IoT network design
  3. Compare network access and management methods — site-to-site and client VPN, SSH, GUI, API, console, jump box, in-band and out-of-band
  4. Use SNMP, syslog, flow data, packet capture, and baseline metrics to assess network health and locate a problem
  5. Explain the purpose of organizational documentation and processes — diagrams, IPAM, change management, life cycle, SOPs, policies, and agreements
Technical Overview

Unit 4 addresses objectives 2.3 (wireless devices and technologies: 802.11 standards, 2.4/5/6 GHz bands, channels and channel width, antennas, SSID and BSSID, autonomous versus controller-based APs, mesh, guest and captive-portal networks, WPA2/WPA3, PSK and Enterprise authentication), 3.5 (network access and management: site-to-site and client-to-site VPN, clientless VPN, split and full tunnel, SSH, GUI, API, console, jump box, in-band and out-of-band management), 3.2 (monitoring: SNMP agents, traps, MIBs and community strings, flow data, packet capture, baselines, log aggregation, syslog, SIEM, API integration, port mirroring), and 3.1 (organizational processes: documentation, IPAM, life cycle, change and configuration management, SOPs, policies, and agreements).

In Plain Terms

Wi-Fi is radio, and radio obeys physics: walls absorb it, neighbors interfere with it, and two access points on the same channel shout over each other. Remote access is how a technician reaches equipment that is somewhere else. Monitoring is how you know something broke before the user calls. Documentation is how the next person fixes it without you. A help desk that has all four closes tickets in minutes; one that has none of them spends the day guessing.

Why We Start Here

You now have a wired, routed, switched network from Units 1–3. This unit adds the radio edge of it and then steps back to how professionals run the whole thing. It sits before security (Unit 5) and troubleshooting (Unit 6) because both of those depend on reading logs, monitoring data, and network diagrams — the tools this unit introduces.

CompTIA Network+ Passes at 720

The exam is scored on a 100–900 scale and passes at 720. Because the scale starts at 100 rather than zero, 720 works out to about 78% of raw points. That is a high bar: a student who treats 70% on a practice set as “good enough” fails the real exam. This course’s mastery gate of 800 exists so that your exam day has a cushion.
