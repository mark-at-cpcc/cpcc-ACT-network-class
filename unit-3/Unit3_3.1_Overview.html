This is the week the course is built around. Subnetting is the single most-failed skill on Network+, and routing and switching are the “given a scenario” objectives the exam leans on hardest. It is also the heaviest reading week — plan for it now, and use the halfway check-in in 3.9 honestly.

Learning Objectives

After completing this unit, you will be able to:

  1. Subnet an IPv4 network with CIDR and VLSM — mask, network, broadcast, usable range, and host count — on paper and on a device
  2. Explain how a router forwards a packet using its routing table, and compare static, default, and dynamic routing
  3. Compare RIP, OSPF, EIGRP, and BGP by type, metric, and administrative distance, and describe FHRP and NAT/PAT
  4. Configure VLANs, 802.1Q trunking, native VLANs, and inter-VLAN routing, and explain why spanning tree exists
  5. Configure interface settings — speed, duplex, link aggregation, PoE, MTU, jumbo frames — and explain the effect of each
  6. Troubleshoot an IP addressing problem from ipconfig and show output: wrong mask, wrong gateway, duplicate address, no route
Technical Overview

Unit 3 addresses the subnetting half of objective 1.7 (CIDR, VLSM, subnet masks, network and broadcast addresses, host counts), objective 2.1 (routing technologies: static, dynamic, and default routes; RIP, OSPF, EIGRP, BGP; administrative distance and metrics; FHRP and virtual IPs; NAT, PAT, and subinterfaces), and objective 2.2 (switching: VLANs and the VLAN database, SVIs, 802.1Q tagging and trunks, native VLANs, voice VLANs, link aggregation, speed and duplex, spanning tree, MTU and jumbo frames). Secondary coverage: 5.3 (troubleshooting IP and switching), 4.3 (port security and device hardening).

In Plain Terms

Subnetting is cutting one block of addresses into smaller blocks so that each department, floor, or device type gets its own. Routing is how traffic finds its way between those blocks. Switching is how it moves inside one. A technician who can subnet in their head can look at 10.4.7.200/27 and know at once which machines it can talk to directly — and that skill closes tickets that leave everyone else rebooting things.

Why We Start Here

Last unit taught you to read an address. This unit teaches you to compute with one, which is why the two are a week apart. Routing and switching follow immediately, because a VLAN is a subnet and a route is a path between subnets: you cannot understand either without the math. Every unit after this assumes it.

The math decays fast without practice, so from here on every class opens with a five-minute Subnet Warm-Up (in 3.6), and the 3.A Subnetting Reference Card stays at your elbow until you stop needing it.

CompTIA Network+ Passes at 720

The exam is scored on a 100–900 scale and passes at 720. Because the scale starts at 100 rather than zero, 720 works out to about 78% of raw points. That is a high bar: a student who treats 70% on a practice set as “good enough” fails the real exam. This course’s mastery gate of 800 exists so that your exam day has a cushion.
